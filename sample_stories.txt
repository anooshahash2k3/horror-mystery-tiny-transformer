The grandfather clock in the hallway struck thirteen before the power went out. Detective Vance struck a match and saw muddy footprints leading straight toward the locked cellar door. Nobody had lived in the house for twelve years.

At midnight the lighthouse beam stopped sweeping the rocks. Mara picked up the brass receiver and heard slow, rhythmic knocking on the other side of the line. From the fog outside came the unmistakable sound of heavy boots on wet stone.

Every portrait in the study had its eyes scratched out. Clara opened the iron ledger on the desk and found her own name written in fresh ink under tomorrow's date. Behind her, the window latch clicked softly.

Inspector Hale examined the sealed envelope left on the library table. Inside was a brass key tagged Room 404 and a train ticket punched for a station that had burned down in nineteen-eighty-two. Rain tapped steadily against the stained glass.

Down in the archive vault, the cassette recorder clicked on by itself. A whispered voice read out three coordinates before the tape snapped. When Elena checked the map, all three points formed a triangle around her own apartment building.

The fog rolled across Blackwood Pier just as the last ferry departed. On the bench sat an open leather suitcase containing a pocket watch frozen at 2:17 AM and a photograph taken moments ago from behind the ticket booth.
